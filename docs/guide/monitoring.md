# Monitoring and intercom

How performers hear themselves on stage, and how the crew talks during a show.

## Floor wedges

Floor wedges are speakers on the stage floor, facing the performers. They play an AUX mix, not
the main mix.

1. Place a **Floor Wedge Monitor** in front of the performer, facing them.
2. Cable the console's **AUX 1** socket (rear side) to an amplifier input, and the amplifier OUT
   to the wedge's **INPUT** with a speaker cable. AUX 1 can also go straight to the wedge.
3. On each channel strip, turn the **AUX 1** knob for how much of that channel goes to the wedge.
   The singer usually wants more of their own voice.
4. The **AUX 1** fader in the master section sets the wedge's overall level.
5. Turn the wedge on and set its coverage on its back panel. Keep it small so the audience
   doesn't hear the monitors.

AUX is sent before the channel fader: pulling a fader down for the audience doesn't change the
monitors. MUTE silences the channel everywhere. AUX 2 is a second, independent mix.

## In-ear monitors

In-ear monitors are heard only by the player carrying the earpiece, so each performer can have a
private mix.

1. Place an **In-Ear Monitor Transmitter** and cable a console AUX into its **INPUT**. Turn it on.
2. Press **−** or **+** on its front to choose a channel (1-16; it shows on the display).
3. Give the performer an **In-Ear Monitor Earpiece** set to the same channel (right-click the
   earpiece to change it). It works anywhere in the inventory.
4. For a second performer with a different mix, use another transmitter on another channel, fed
   from AUX 2.

Example: singer on channel 1 from AUX 1, drummer on channel 2 from AUX 2.

## Intercom

The intercom lets the crew (director, stage manager, lighting, sound) talk to each other through
headsets during the show. The audience never hears it, and it doesn't go through the console.

1. Place an **Intercom Base Station** and turn it on (back panel). Its coverage knob (16-128
   blocks) sets how far from it the beltpacks work.
2. Give every crew member an **Intercom Beltpack**. Right-click it to choose channel **A** or
   **B**.
3. To talk, **sneak + right-click** the beltpack to turn **TALK** on, and speak normally. Everyone
   carrying a beltpack on your channel hears you, wherever they are inside the station's
   coverage. Turn TALK off to listen only.
4. Optional: cable a console AUX (or MAIN) into the station's **PGM IN**. Every beltpack near the
   station then hears the show as well, so the crew can follow it.

Two channels let two teams work at once, for example A for stage management and B for lighting.
With TALK on, players nearby still hear you through normal voice chat, as with any voice.
